"LLM agents searching your Gmail make too many queries, because they search by matching words."

The loop: search, zero hits, rewrite the query, search again, open email after email. Every round costs tokens, and when the answer isn't there it keeps looking.

"The words the model generates are often not in the email." It searches "coding competition"; the email says "Codeforces Round 1043". So it misses, or it returns the HackerRank job test because it says "coding".

"Keyword search found nothing for 7 of our 12 meaning-based questions. Not wrong answers. Nothing."

So we built Inbox Atlas.

A retrieval layer that searches by meaning and returns a decision, for agents and people.

"Here is the whole thing end to end."

Ingest: Gmail read-only and Obsidian notes, cleaned, embedded locally by atlas-embed, our own encoder, and stored in Tiger Cloud with a pgvector index.

Query: a question comes from an agent over MCP, iMessage, voice or the web. "Grok writes the facets in the words emails actually use, plus what to exclude." We score every email against that region; a hub-corrected score answers "is it here at all?". Then we hand back only the sentences that answer, under a token budget, or "nothing here, stop".

Why it is more accurate

The words a model would search for are often not in the email. Word matching misses them. Meaning does not.

coding competitionfindsCodeforces Round 1043

"17 times fewer tokens on email questions, and more accurate."

On the 12 demo inbox questions: keyword search hands the agent 3,724 tokens per question and gets 33% right. Atlas hands over 225 tokens and gets 58% right. Finding the right emails: Recall@10 0.175 vs 0.867.

"Why more accurate? The related words the model would search for often aren't in the email, so word matching misses them, and meaning doesn't."

One server for Cursor, Claude Code, Hermes and OpenClaw.

Cursor CLI

Built and routed with it. In our own testing outside this repo, Cursor CLI was the most token-efficient at routing compared with Claude Code, Codex and Grok.

"Under it: our own encoder, frozen backbone, LoRA, a frozen teacher, trained overnight on a quarter million emails on one GPU."

atlas-embed: frozen bge-base with 1.18 million trainable LoRA parameters, Matryoshka heads (768, 256, 64), listwise distillation from a frozen cross-encoder teacher. 224,867 Enron emails, 19,980 Grok labels, 491 thousand teacher scores, one RTX 4070 SUPER overnight. Vague queries nDCG@10 0.692 vs 0.635. The region's yes or no is hub corrected: each email is compared with what it scores on random topic sets of the same size.

Stack: Grok for facets, the agent, speech to text and realtime voice. Tiger Cloud: pgvector HNSW, TimescaleDB query log, chat history. Photon Spectrum for iMessage. MCP for every agent.

Say it exactly: "In our own testing outside this repo, Cursor CLI was the most token-efficient at routing compared with Claude Code, Codex and Grok." No numbers.

Inside the model.

"Left: the encoder. The bge-base backbone is frozen; we only train small LoRA adapters on each attention layer, 1.18 million parameters, and it learns from a frozen cross-encoder teacher."

"Right: the region. Grok's facet list goes through the frozen encoder into a Set Transformer that outputs four anchors and a boundary, calibrated into a probability: is this email inside the question or not."

Gray hatched means frozen, red means trained. Matryoshka heads let the same embedding work at 768, 256 or 64 dims.
